Banksy: The Anonymous Artist

Banksy is an anonymous British street artist known for his distinctive stencil technique and provocative visual messages. His work combines humour, irony and social criticism to explore themes such as war, politics, consumerism, surveillance and inequality.

Emerging from the Bristol graffiti scene, Banksy became internationally recognised for transforming public spaces into platforms for artistic expression and social commentary. His work can be found in murals, prints, installations and other artistic formats.

Although his identity remains publicly unconfirmed, his work has become one of the most recognisable forms of contemporary street art.

Street Art Locations

Girl with Balloon

2002 · London, United Kingdom · Hope and innocence

A young girl reaches towards a red, heart-shaped balloon. The image is widely associated with hope, loss and the fragility of innocence.

Love Is in the Air (Flower Thrower)

2003 · Bethlehem area · Peace and conflict

A masked figure appears ready to throw an object, but holds a bouquet of flowers instead. The image contrasts the language of protest and violence with a symbol of peace.

Kissing Coppers

2004 · Brighton, United Kingdom · Identity and authority

Two uniformed police officers kiss. By placing an intimate gesture within a symbol of authority, the work invites reflection on social conventions, masculinity and public perceptions of policing.

Napalm

2004 · Screenprint · War and consumerism

The print places a frightened child from a famous Vietnam War photograph between Mickey Mouse and Ronald McDonald. The contrast challenges the cheerful imagery of commercial culture against the reality of war.

One Nation Under CCTV

2007–2008 · London · Surveillance

A child appears to paint a message about surveillance while a police officer observes nearby. The work draws attention to the tension between public space, authority and constant monitoring.

Season’s Greetings

2018 · Port Talbot, Wales · Pollution

A child seems to enjoy falling snow, until the other side of the artwork reveals that the flakes are ash from a burning bin. The work connects childhood imagery with industrial pollution.

Valentine’s Day Mascara

2023 · Margate, England · Domestic violence

A 1950s-style housewife with a bruised face appears beside a freezer containing a man’s legs. The unsettling scene has been widely interpreted as a commentary on domestic violence against women.

No Ball Games

2009 · London · Public space and childhood

Children play with a street sign that prohibits ball games. The scene can be read as a playful challenge to restrictions on children’s use of public space.

Laugh Now

2002 · Artwork series · Power and society

A row of monkeys wearing signs with a warning about the future reverses the expected hierarchy between humans and animals. The work uses dark humour to question power, human behaviour and social order.
